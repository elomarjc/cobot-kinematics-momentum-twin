🎯 The Industrial Problem Solved

Collaborative robots work directly alongside human operators without safety fences. Under ISO/TS 15066 safety standards, the robot must immediately halt motion if human collision occurs. Adding external 6-axis force-torque load cells or tactile skins increases robot cost and cable failures. Furthermore, approaching kinematic singularities causes standard inverse kinematics to command infinite joint speeds, causing overspeed trips.

💡 How This Digital Twin Solves It

Implements analytical Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) kinematics, Yoshikawa manipulability tracking, Damped Least-Squares (DLS) singularity-robust inverse kinematics to bound joint velocities (≤ 2.5 rad/s), and a sensorless Generalized Momentum Disturbance Observer that estimates external contact wrenches directly from motor currents and joint positions in <15 ms.

📊 Telemetry & Control Glossary

  • Damped Least-Squares (DLS): Singularity-robust inverse kinematics solver that regularizes (J J^T + λ^2 I)^-1 to prevent joint overspeed trips near kinematic boundaries.
  • Yoshikawa Manipulability: Scalar measure w(q) = sqrt(det(J*J^T)) indicating kinematic dexterity; approaches zero near mechanical singular configurations.
  • Generalized Momentum Observer: Sensorless disturbance estimator p = M(q)*q_dot that isolates external collision wrenches from motor currents without requiring external wrist force sensors.
